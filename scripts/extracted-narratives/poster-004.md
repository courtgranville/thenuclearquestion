# Poster 004: Most of Our Energy Isn't Electricity

- **Source:** `004-most-of-our-energy-isnt-electricity.pdf`, draft dated 24 April 2026 (longer-prose version, pre-trim).
- **Cross-reference:** `version 2/004-version2.pdf`, dated 1 May 2026 (shipped trim).
- **Extracted:** 2026-05-15

The print draft and the shipped print share the title *Most of Our Energy Isn't Electricity* and the subtitle *UK final energy consumption in 2024, by carrier and end-use sector*. This is also what the site `posterData.ts` carries.

This is the most heavily trimmed poster in the series. The shipped v2 print cut **three full subsidiary charts** that the print draft carries: a 1970–2024 final-energy time-series, a 1970–2026 retail-energy-price chart, and a 1990–2026 carbon-intensity chart. Each has its own body prose. These are by far the strongest editorial threads available for promotion to the web.

## Headline / opener

**Print draft, verbatim (two paragraphs):**

> When people talk about the clean energy transition, they usually mean the electricity system - wind farms, solar panels, nuclear reactors, new grid connections. It is easy to assume that if we fix how electricity is made, we have fixed most of the problem. We haven't. Electricity is only 18% of the energy the UK uses. The other 82% - shown in the four large clusters branching from the centre of this diagram - is fossil fuel burned directly: petrol in cars, jet fuel in aeroplanes, gas in boilers, oil for shipping, coal and gas for industrial heat. In 2024 the UK used 1,542 terawatt-hours of final energy. Petroleum alone accounted for 47%; natural gas another 28%.

> Decarbonising how we generate electricity only decarbonises the fraction of the system already running on it. Everything else still burns. The scale of the transformation required is visible here, in the proportions of this diagram.

## Subsidiary charts (PRINT-ONLY — cut from shipped v2)

These three small-multiples sit on the right-hand side of the print poster, each with its own headline, axis label, and body block. None of them appear in the shipped print.

### Chart 1 — "Our energy system has transformed before"

- **Sub-headline:** UK final energy consumption by carrier, 1970–2024
- **Axes:** y-axis 0–900 TWh; x-axis 1970–2025 Year
- **Body prose, verbatim:**

> It wouldn't be the first time. In 1970 coal supplied more than a third of UK energy and petroleum supplied almost half. Then North Sea gas arrived, and over two decades coal collapsed — almost entirely replaced by gas. But electricity's share of total energy barely moved, creeping from 11% to 18% across fifty-four years. The carriers can change — that much has happened before. What has never happened is the rapid growth of electricity as a share of total energy use. That is what has to happen next.

### Chart 2 — "All retail energy tracks global fossil markets"

- **Sub-headline:** UK retail energy prices by fuel, 1970–2026, all in pence per kWh
- **Axes:** y-axis 0–35 Pence per kWh; x-axis 1970–2030 Year
- **Body prose, verbatim:**

> Look at the lines: they move together. Every oil shock of the past fifty years - 1973, 1979, 2008, 2022 - shows up on all five fuels simultaneously. That is because British retail energy is priced, ultimately, against global fossil markets, no matter which carrier a household chooses. Electricity has always been the most expensive per unit. Gas has always been the cheapest. But all five rise and fall together, because British energy is still fossil energy, and fossil energy has one price.

### Chart 3 — "Only one carrier is decarbonising at speed"

- **Sub-headline:** Carbon intensity of UK final energy carriers, 1990–2026, gCO₂ per kWh at point of combustion
- **Axes:** y-axis 0–800 gCO2 per kWh; x-axis 1990–2030 Year
- **Body prose, verbatim:**

> Petroleum, natural gas, solid fuel, heat, bioenergy - their emissions per unit of fuel burned barely change from decade to decade, because the chemistry of combustion doesn't change. A litre of petrol releases the same carbon in 2026 as it did in 1990. Engines and boilers have become more efficient, which reduces how much fuel we need to burn - but the fuel itself is unchanged. One line falls steeply. In 1990 the UK grid emitted over 700 grams of CO₂ per kilowatt-hour, burning mostly coal. Over three decades that fell by more than 70%. The electricity line crossed beneath coal in 2016, beneath petroleum in 2020, and is closing in on natural gas. Every other carrier is effectively stuck at its chemistry. Electricity is the only one with a direction of travel.

These three blocks together amount to roughly 380 words of prose that does not appear on the shipped print at all - and they are the strongest editorial threads in the series for promotion to the web.

## Methodology block

Print draft, under heading **Assumptions:**, five sub-strands, verbatim:

> **Scope:** All figures show UK final energy consumption - the energy delivered to end users - not primary energy. Primary energy would add ~50% to the gas and coal share, reflecting conversion losses in power stations before electricity reaches the grid.

> **Electricity's origin:** Electricity is shown as a carrier where it is consumed. In 2024 the UK grid was roughly 30% gas, 30% wind, 13% nuclear, 13% biomass, 11% imports, 4% solar. How electricity is generated is the subject of Poster 001.

> **Emissions basis (Chart 3):** Fossil fuel figures are combustion emissions from DESNZ 2025 GHG conversion factors (gross calorific value basis). Electricity figures are the UK grid average, which changes annually with the generation mix. Lifecycle (well-to-tank) emissions would add roughly 15-25% to each fossil fuel line but would not change their flat shape.

> Bioenergy is counted at zero combustion emissions under standard UK inventory accounting, reflecting the biogenic nature of the carbon. Lifecycle accounting would give a non-zero value.

> **Excluded from the dendrogram:** The energy industry's own consumption (~105 TWh) and international aviation and shipping bunker fuels, per DUKES convention.

Print draft data strap (foot of poster), verbatim:

> Data: Digest of UK Energy Statistics (DUKES) 2025, Tables 1.1.1, 1.1.3 and 1.1.5. Retail energy prices: DESNZ Quarterly Energy Prices; ONS RPI series CZMO; DESNZ Weekly Road Fuel Prices. Carbon intensity: UK grid from Ember Yearly Electricity Data and Carbon Brief analysis of DESNZ/NESO figures; fuel combustion factors from 2025 DESNZ GHG Conversion Factors for Company Reporting. Visualisation: Court Granville, 2026.

Shipped v2 print collapses methodology to two short sentences:

> Final energy consumption (energy delivered to end users), per DUKES convention. Primary energy would be ~50% larger. Non-energy use shown as where petroleum physically goes; not combusted.

And the shipped data strap is shortened to:

> Data: Digest of UK Energy Statistics (DUKES) 2025, Tables 1.1.1, 1.1.3 and 1.1.5. Visualisation: Court Granville, 2026.

(Because v2 cut Charts 2 and 3, the retail-prices and carbon-intensity data sources are dropped from the strap. On the web, if those charts are restored, the longer strap is the one to use.)

## Declared weakness ("what this can't tell us")

This poster does not carry a single "weakness" block - the honesty work is distributed across five methodology sub-strands. The strongest single declaration is:

> **Scope:** All figures show UK final energy consumption - the energy delivered to end users - not primary energy. Primary energy would add ~50% to the gas and coal share, reflecting conversion losses in power stations before electricity reaches the grid.

And on bioenergy:

> Bioenergy is counted at zero combustion emissions under standard UK inventory accounting, reflecting the biogenic nature of the carbon. Lifecycle accounting would give a non-zero value.

Both declare a framing choice the poster has made and the magnitude of the difference if the alternative framing were used. That is exactly the Criterion 7 disclosure pattern. The `posterSources.004.caveat` on the site already paraphrases the primary-vs-final-energy point.

## Editorial threads not present in the print

Items below are in the longer print draft but were cut from the shipped v2 print. The three subsidiary charts are the biggest items in the whole series.

1. **Chart 1: "Our energy system has transformed before."** A 1970–2024 time-series of UK final energy by carrier. Body prose (the "North Sea gas arrived, and over two decades coal collapsed" walk-through) argues that the carrier mix has shifted before, but electricity's share has barely moved - and that is what has to change next. This is the print's argument for *historical feasibility*. The site does not currently carry any historical view of UK final energy; the chart and its prose are the easiest historical anchor available. Useful as a SectionFrame block below the main dendrogram.

2. **Chart 2: "All retail energy tracks global fossil markets."** A 1970–2026 retail-price chart in p/kWh covering all carriers. Body prose argues that British retail energy is priced against global fossil markets regardless of which carrier a household chooses, so "all five rise and fall together, because British energy is still fossil energy, and fossil energy has one price." This is the print's argument for *price feasibility* - it is the closest the series comes to addressing affordability directly. Strong candidate for promotion because the affordability question is otherwise absent from the website.

3. **Chart 3: "Only one carrier is decarbonising at speed."** A 1990–2026 carbon-intensity chart in gCO₂/kWh covering all carriers. Body prose: "A litre of petrol releases the same carbon in 2026 as it did in 1990... The electricity line crossed beneath coal in 2016, beneath petroleum in 2020, and is closing in on natural gas. Every other carrier is effectively stuck at its chemistry. Electricity is the only one with a direction of travel." This is the print's argument for *why electrification* - it ties poster 004 back to poster 001 and forward to the next stage of the thesis. The single strongest pull-quote candidate in the whole series.

4. **The fuller methodology block.** Five named sub-strands (Scope / Electricity's origin / Emissions basis / Bioenergy / Excluded from the dendrogram). The shipped print kept only Scope and bioenergy in compressed form. The web has room for all five. The "Excluded from the dendrogram (~105 TWh)" sub-strand in particular is a good marker of intellectual honesty - it names exactly what is missing and why.

5. **The "Electricity's origin" cross-reference to Poster 001.** "How electricity is generated is the subject of Poster 001." Print only as prose. Useful on the web because the site does not currently cross-link between posters; one explicit reference here would reinforce the sequence.

6. **The v2-only canvas callout.** "Electricity is just 18% of UK final energy. Decarbonising how it's made only cleans this slice. Everything else needs to be electrified before it can be decarbonised at all." This is the shipped print's strongest sentence and does not appear in the print draft. The web should carry this regardless of which body prose is used.

- **Expandable "More on this" disclosure below the viz:** The full methodology block - all five sub-strands. The current `posterSources.004.caveat` covers the primary-vs-final-energy point; the other four sub-strands are not currently on the web.
- **On the Sources page:** Promote the full print data strap (DUKES + DESNZ Quarterly Energy Prices + ONS RPI + Ember + Carbon Brief + DESNZ GHG factors). If the subsidiary charts are not promoted, only the DUKES part of the strap is needed.

Currently `posterData.ts.pullQuote` uses "Decarbonising how we generate electricity only decarbonises the fraction of the system already running on it. Everything else still burns." Both work; this is a Court decision.
